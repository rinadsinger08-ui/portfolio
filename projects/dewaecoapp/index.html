DEWAEcoApp

A sustainability prototype developed for the DIDI Design Competition, December 2024–June 2025. The concept rewards sustainable purchases and reduced water or electricity use with DEWA points. The recovered calculator interface compares water-consuming products and their long-term costs.

Read the source ↗Project notes

The rewards concept

Two proposed ways to earn DEWA points.

SUSTAINABLE PURCHASES

Choose a greener product

Sign in and buy sustainable-labelled products or recommended sustainable substitutes through participating retailers to earn DEWA points.

LOWER UTILITY USE

Compare monthly bills

Scan a water or electricity bill. The proposed app compares its consumption with the previous month and awards DEWA points when water or electricity usage decreases.

These are proposed product features. The recovered source demonstrates simulated reward flows; working sign-in, purchase verification, bill scanning, monthly usage comparison, and real DEWA point issuance are not established by the available code.

My contribution

Built the React and TypeScript calculator interface, product cost comparisons, and state-driven add/edit/remove interactions. Prototyped conservation rewards and partner-account flows within the broader sustainability concept.

IMPLEMENTED IN THE SOURCE

Water-cost comparison

  • Add, edit, and remove typed product records.
  • Adjust daily usage and water price.
  • Calculate monthly, annual, and five-year costs.
  • Compare product costs in a shared table.

SIMULATED CONCEPTS

Reward flows

  • Receipt text prompt simulates scanning.
  • Partner buttons toggle interface state.
  • Purchase checks use an example purchase.
  • No real OCR, retailer login, or API verification.

How the calculation works

Monthly cost = flow rate × daily usage minutes × 30 days × water rate. Annual cost = monthly cost × 12. Five-year total = product price + annual cost × 5.

The source uses gallons per minute and USD per gallon. These are editable prototype assumptions; it does not fetch live utility tariffs.

Source status & next steps

Next steps are to recover those files, improve numeric validation and input labels, and add build checks. This case study preserves the original implementation without claiming the simulated integrations are production features.
